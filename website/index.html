The Pivot Aide year

For us, tax season lasts twelve months.

Here is what a year with us actually looks like. Note where filing sits — at the end, as the report on work already done.

Jan – Apr

Filing season

The year’s decisions become a number. If we did the other nine months properly, nothing here is a surprise.

  • Returns prepared and e-filed
  • Extensions where documents are late
  • Prior-year catch-up and amendments
  • K-1s chased before they hold you up
May – Jul

The reset

The quietest quarter and the most valuable one. Everything structural is cheaper to change now than in December.

  • Entity and S-corp election review
  • Reasonable compensation set
  • W-4 and quarterly estimates tuned
  • Books cleaned and brought current
Aug – Oct

Projection

We run your year forward and tell you the number before it is final, while there is still time to move it.

  • Mid-year projection with a written figure
  • Retirement plan design and funding
  • Equipment and depreciation timing
  • Multi-state nexus review
Nov – Dec

The closing window

The last moves that still count. After 31 December almost every lever is gone, whoever you hire in March.

  • Income and expense timing
  • Retirement and HSA contributions
  • Charitable and loss harvesting
  • Payroll, bonuses and distributions set
Why this matters

By January, most of your tax bill is already decided. A preparer who meets you in February can only report what happened. That is a real service, and we do it well — but it is the last step, not the whole job.

Quick FAQs

Three we get every week.

Do I need Self-Employed if I have a small 1099 side gig?
Yes — any self-employment income on Schedule C falls under Self-Employed. Rental income on Schedule E starts at Premium. We’ll confirm before you pay.
What counts as a “basic” K-1?
A straightforward partnership or S-corp K-1 without special allocations, multi-state apportionment, or complex basis tracking. We flag anything complex upfront, before it changes your price.
Can I add state returns or fix an old one?
Absolutely — we file in all fifty states, and we can file past years or amend returns if needed, priced after a quick scope check.
