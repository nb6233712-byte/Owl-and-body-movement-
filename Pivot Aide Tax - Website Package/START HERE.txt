THE APP, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
-------------------------

Working name: The File Room. Clients upload documents, sign Form 8879,
read the finished return, ask questions where the documents already
are, see their own deadlines, and pay. It does not compute tax and it
does not generate advice - every number a client sees was put there by
a person at the firm. Web first, then native iPhone and Android.

You asked for a custom app rather than a licensed platform, and the

THE BRAND CHARACTER
-------------------

The owl is Uncle Pat. The home page now reads "Uncle Sam looks out for
the government. Uncle Pat looks out for you." That division of labour
is the core positioning and it runs through the site and the app.

He now wears a white collar and a gold tie. The change is not
decoration: a collar gives him shoulders and shoulders give him a job,
which turns an owl shape into a portrait of somebody you would hand
your documents to. The tie uses the brand gold already on the beak, so
the accent runs on a single vertical line - beak, knot, blade.

He is live in both prototypes now - the site header, the home page
hero, the Meet Uncle Pat section, and the app's title bar and message
threads. The "placeholder art" labels are gone, because it is no longer
placeholder art. Item 7 is the mark sheet; item 9 is the artwork.

Exhibit J of the strategy file (item 3) still holds the wider art
direction - five poses and the illustration brief. This mark answers
the primary pose. The other four are still to be drawn.


THE STANDING FILE
-----------------

The flagship engagement is on the Tax Strategy section: the year-round
package for high-income individuals and business owners - a dedicated
advisor, four scheduled strategy sessions a year, a written strategy
plan, a mid-year projection, unlimited questions, and every return,
estimate and notice handled inside one annual fee. Two tracks: A for
high-income individuals, B for business owners and entities.

No price is published. It is quoted after a free 45-minute scoping call
and a Second Look at three prior returns.

A NOTE ON FONTS
---------------

Both prototypes use three web fonts (Fraunces, Poppins, JetBrains Mono)
loaded from Google Fonts. On a computer with internet they load
automatically. With no internet, the pages fall back to system fonts -
layout, colours and content are unaffected, only the lettering changes
slightly. Nothing breaks either way.


STILL OPEN - DECISIONS ONLY YOU CAN MAKE
----------------------------------------

From the website work:

  1. Which Circular 230 credential covers IRS representation, and whose
     name signs the Form 2848. This gates the whole Audit & Resolution
     section and The Standing File. Do not publish either until this is
     answered - and the app's service catalogue cannot list what the
     firm cannot perform.

  2. Business return pricing. The prototype shows $750 and $1,250; the
     live site currently shows $550 for both. Proposed, not confirmed.

  3. The legal standing of Pivot Aide Tax - its own registered entity,
     or an assumed name of Pivot Aide Consulting.

From the app work (full list in Exhibit K of the build plan):

  4. Is this a product or a private portal? It changes the data model
     on day one and cannot be deferred.

  5. Who is the Qualified Individual? The FTC Safeguards Rule requires
     a named person accountable for the security programme.

  6. Who builds it - an agency, a contract team, or a hire.

  7. Signing vendor: DocuSign at $2.40 per identity check, or SafeSend
     behind the app. SafeSend does not publish pricing.

  8. Do you want to surcharge card payments? CPACharge supports it out
     of the box; Stripe leaves you to build it. This decides the
     processor.

  9. App store commission - confirm in writing with Apple and Google
     that tax preparation counts as a service consumed outside the app
     before building in-app payment for the phone apps. A 15-30%
     commission on preparation fees would break the model.

 10. The name. "The File Room" is a working title chosen to sit beside
     The Standing File. It is yours to overrule.


A NOTE ON THE FIGURES
---------------------

Tax figures on the Tax Resources section are for tax year 2026 and come
from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, IRS Notice IR-2025-111, Revenue
Procedure 2025-19 and the 2026 standard mileage announcement. They will
need refreshing when the IRS publishes the 2027 numbers.

Every rule and price in the build plan was checked on 2 September 2026
and is sourced in Exhibit L. Three figures rest on secondary sources
and are flagged there rather than relied on: SafeSend's per-return
pricing, the vulnerability-scanning cost, and the 15-20% maintenance
rule of thumb.
